Remotion & Motion Graphics

Remotion vs libass: Choosing a Motion Graphics Renderer for Automated Video

Remotion rendered at 11 seconds per second of video on our hardware, so automated news graphics moved to libass. A measured comparison.

News AI Engine Editorial Team November 21, 2026 · 4 min read

We like Remotion. We use it for explainer videos where every scene is designed by hand. But when we built automated news production, we chose a much older tool for motion graphics: libass, the subtitle renderer built into FFmpeg. This article explains the decision with numbers, so you can make the right call for your own project.

What each tool is

Remotion renders videos from React components. You describe each frame with JSX and CSS; a headless Chromium browser screenshots every frame; FFmpeg encodes the frames. You get the full power of the web platform: layout, gradients, SVG, fonts, 3D transforms and any JavaScript animation library.

libass renders Advanced SubStation Alpha (ASS) subtitles. ASS was designed for anime fansubs and is far more capable than its name suggests: per-character timing, transforms over time, vector drawing, clipping masks, blur, borders and movement. FFmpeg can burn ASS onto video in the same pass that encodes it.

The measurement that decided it

On the same Android phone (running Linux tools through Termux and a proot Ubuntu environment):

Renderer Speed 60-second short 5-video batch
Remotion (headless Chromium, 2 workers) ~11 s per 1 s of video ~11 minutes ~1 hour for graphics alone
libass via FFmpeg graphics drawn during the final encode adds well under a minute minutes

Remotion also had to share roughly 2 GB of free memory with everything else the engine does. For a pipeline that produces several videos in one batch, an extra hour of rendering per five videos was not acceptable.

On a desktop with a GPU, Remotion would be far faster. The decision is about your hardware and volume, not about which tool is “better”.

What libass can actually do

The question we had to answer was whether libass could produce graphics that look professionally designed rather than like karaoke subtitles. These features made it possible:

Need ASS feature
Typing effect one event per character (or per-character alpha changes) on spoken timing
Pop-in with overshoot \t(0,110,\fscx112\fscy112)\t(110,200,\fscx100\fscy100)
Motion blur feel animate \blur8 to \blur0 while moving
Wipes and masked reveals animate a rectangular \clip(...) with \t
Lower thirds, boxes, pills vector drawing with \p1 paths
Arrows, circles, cursor custom drawing paths, animated with \move
Slides and whips \move(x1,y1,x2,y2,t1,t2)
Consistent typography bundled fonts via ass=file.ass:fontsdir=...

With a small library of builder functions, the engine can produce headline cards, name supers, numbered development boards, typing windows, kinetic text, location pins, timelines, comparison cards and stat cards - all from Python. See Animating News Graphics With ASS Subtitles for code.

Where Remotion still wins

  • Complex layout. Flexbox, text wrapping and responsive layout are effortless in the browser; in ASS you measure text yourself.
  • Rich visuals. Gradients, shadows with spread, images inside graphics, 3D perspective, particle effects.
  • Designer workflow. You can preview and scrub in Remotion Studio and iterate visually.
  • Reusable components. React components compose naturally.

For hand-crafted explainers, product videos and anything where each scene is unique, Remotion is the better tool.

Where libass wins

  • Speed. Graphics cost almost nothing extra in the encode you were already doing.
  • Low resource use. No browser, no Node.js runtime during rendering.
  • Single pass. Footage, graphics and audio are composed in one FFmpeg command.
  • Deterministic output. A text file in, the same video out.

Lessons from making libass look good

Measure text yourself. ASS does not lay text out for you. We measure strings with the same font files in Pillow and calibrate against what libass actually renders: on our fonts, libass drew text about 0.81-0.89 times the width Pillow predicted. Without that calibration, headlines wrapped too early and boxes did not fit.

Balance line breaks. Greedy wrapping leaves orphans (“…Across / Nigeria”). After wrapping, we shrink the target width while the number of lines stays the same, which evens out the lines.

Leave room for descenders in masks. A wipe implemented with a clip rectangle exactly one line tall cuts the bottoms off “g”, “p” and “y”. Make masks taller than the line.

Use a design system, not ad-hoc styles. One palette, two typefaces and a fixed set of layouts per format keep hundreds of generated videos consistent.

Our recommendation

  • Producing many similar videos automatically on modest hardware? Use libass through FFmpeg, with a small graphics library.
  • Producing fewer, highly designed videos, or you have a GPU machine? Use Remotion.
  • Doing both? Do what we do: Remotion for hand-made explainers, libass for the automated pipeline - and share the design language (fonts, colours, timing rules) between them.
