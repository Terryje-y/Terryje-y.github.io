Choosing a Video Editing Style for Your News Channel: Dynamic, Typing or Broadcast

Three proven editing styles for AI news videos, what each one is good for, and how to pick the right look for TikTok, Shorts and YouTube.

News AI Engine Editorial Team November 21, 2026 · 3 min read
Key takeaways
  • Pick one main style per channel so viewers recognise you instantly.
  • Fast, bold styles suit short vertical video; calmer layouts suit long 16:9 video.
  • Test styles side by side on the same story before you commit.

Two channels can cover exactly the same story and get very different results. Often the difference is not the script or the voice but the editing style: how the video looks and moves. In this guide we compare the three styles used by the News AI Engine and explain how to choose.

Why one consistent style matters

Viewers scroll fast. When your video appears, they should recognise your channel before they read a single word. A consistent style does that. It also makes your channel look planned and trustworthy rather than thrown together.

That does not mean every video must look identical. It means the system stays the same: the same fonts, colours, caption position and type of graphics.

Style 1: Dynamic

What it looks like: a bold headline at the top, fast footage cuts underneath, and animated key-point boards that slide in as the voice mentions each development.

Best for: TikTok and YouTube Shorts, breaking stories, and topics with several developments.

Why it works: the screen changes every few seconds, which holds attention on short-form platforms. The key-point boards help viewers follow a story with several moving parts.

Watch out for: too many effects. Movement should support the story, not compete with it. Our engine’s visual director is deliberately restrained: it adds a graphic only when the story calls for one.

Style 2: Typing

What it looks like: key words and phrases are revealed as if being typed on screen, with a clean, minimal background and footage.

Best for: explainers, quotes that have been verified, and calm, analytical channels.

Why it works: the typing motion draws the eye to the words and gives the video a thoughtful, documentary feel.

Watch out for: long sentences. Typing works best with short phrases that can be read in a second or two.

Style 3: Broadcast

What it looks like: a wide 16:9 newsroom layout with a title card, lower-third name bars and a subtle news ticker area.

Best for: longer YouTube videos, roundups and anything watched on a TV or laptop.

Why it works: viewers already know this visual language from television news, so it instantly feels credible.

Watch out for: small text. Broadcast layouts were designed for big screens, so check that every label is still readable on a phone.

Quick comparison

Style Best format Pace Feel
Dynamic 9:16 vertical Fast Energetic, modern
Typing 9:16 or 16:9 Medium Clean, thoughtful
Broadcast 16:9 wide Calm Classic, credible

How to choose for your channel

Ask yourself three questions:

  1. Where will most people watch? For phones and short-form feeds, start with Dynamic. For YouTube on larger screens, start with Broadcast.
  2. How fast do your stories move? Breaking news suits Dynamic. Explainers suit Typing.
  3. What feeling do you want? Energetic, thoughtful or classic? Your style should match your channel’s personality.

Test before you commit

The best way to decide is to make the same story in two or three styles and compare them. In our engine this is a simple benchmark: one headline, three styles, side by side. Watch them on your phone, show them to a friend, and notice which one you would stop scrolling for.

Once you pick a style, give it a few weeks. Viewers need time to recognise a look, and a style that changes every day never becomes familiar.

Small details that make any style look professional

  • Keep text inside the safe area. Platform buttons and captions cover the edges of vertical video.
  • Use one or two fonts, not five.
  • Limit your colours to your brand colours plus white.
  • Leave space. Crowded screens look cheap; space looks premium.
