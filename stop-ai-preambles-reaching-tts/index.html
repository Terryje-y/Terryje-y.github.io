Stop AI Preambles From Reaching Your Text-to-Speech

Headings, stage directions and 'Here is your script' notes were read aloud by our narrator. The cleaner that keeps only speakable text.

One of the most embarrassing bugs in our engine was invisible in the script editor and obvious to anyone who listened: the synthetic narrator occasionally read out things that were never meant to be spoken.

When we inspected the text actually sent to text-to-speech, we found lines like these at the top of real narration files:

One note before the script: I could not verify the supplied headline...
# BREAKING NEWS SCRIPT
**[SCENE START]**
**HOOK**

A language model writing a “script” naturally formats it like a script - headings, scene markers, citation tags like [S1], and sometimes a polite note to the editor. All of that is useful on screen. None of it belongs in the audio.

Why “just tell the model not to” is not enough

Our prompt already said “Do not explain your writing process” and “Do not include headings in narration”. Models mostly comply, but “mostly” is not acceptable when the output is a published voice-over. The fix has to live in code, after generation and before speech.

What must be removed - and what must stay

We wrote down the rules before writing any regular expressions.

Remove:

  • section headings (## INTRODUCTION, **HOOK**, 1. MAIN INCIDENT, short all-caps title lines);
  • the visual-only “development board” block;
  • stage directions ([SCENE START], [MUSIC], (pause));
  • citation markers ([S1], [Source 2]) and the source-notes list;
  • AI meta lines (“Here is the script”, “As an AI”, “Word count:”, “Job ID:”);
  • a preamble before the first heading, if it reads like a note to the editor.

Keep:

  • every factual qualification inside narration paragraphs - “according to reports”, “if verified”, “the claim has not been confirmed”. These are part of honest journalism, not AI chatter.

That second list is the reason a blunt “delete anything mentioning verification” rule would be wrong.

Detecting headings safely

A heading can look like many things. Our detector normalises a line, then checks it against known section names, Markdown headings, bold-only lines and short all-caps titles:

def is_heading(line):
    raw = line.strip()
    if inline_body(raw):           # "**DEVELOPMENT ONE:** The court adjourned..." is narration
        return False
    key = heading_key(raw)         # strip #, **, numbering, trailing colon -> upper case
    if any(key == n or key.startswith(n + " ") for n in SECTION_NAMES):
        return True
    if raw.startswith("#") or re.fullmatch(r"\*\*[^*]{1,80}\*\*:?", raw):
        return True
    letters = re.sub(r"[^A-Za-z]", "", raw)
    return bool(letters) and letters.isupper() and len(raw.split()) <= 9 and not raw.endswith((".", "?", "!"))

The inline_body check matters. Models sometimes write a label and a full sentence on one line:

**DEVELOPMENT ONE:** The court adjourned the case until next Tuesday, according to the defence.

Deleting that whole line would delete real narration. Instead, if the text after the label is a proper sentence of eight or more words, we keep the sentence and drop only the label.

Meta lines and preambles

Meta text is matched on the line after stripping Markdown, because models often bold their own notes (**Job ID:** …):

META_LINE = re.compile(r"(?i)^\s*(?:(?:one\s+)?note\s*:|here(?:'s| is)\s+(?:the|your)\b.*\bscript|"
                       r"as an ai\b|i (?:could not|cannot) verify|word count\s*:|job id\s*:)")

For text that appears before the first heading, we only treat it as a preamble if it contains editorial vocabulary (“script”, “headline”, “verify”, “source material”). A news story that simply starts without a heading is left alone.

Don’t discard the warnings

The most important design choice: removed meta text is returned, not thrown away.

result = clean_narration(draft)
result["text"]          # what the narrator will read
result["editor_notes"]  # e.g. "I could not verify the supplied headline..."
result["removed"]       # ["preamble", "heading:HOOK", "development_board", ...]

A model saying “I could not verify this headline” is a signal a human editor should see. We save it next to the job and show it in the dashboard. The narrator never says it; the editor never misses it.

A second line of defence in quality control

After rendering, our quality checks run an artifact detector on the final narration text and fail the video if any heading, stage direction, citation marker or meta line survived. That turns a silent embarrassment into a blocked publish with a clear reason.

Testing it on real drafts

We ran the cleaner against every historical draft in the engine. A few results:

  • a 1,700-word draft lost its editorial preamble, a channel-name heading, a “Job ID” line and nine section headings - and kept every narration paragraph;
  • a draft that labelled developments inline kept the sentences and dropped only the labels;
  • drafts with no problems came through unchanged.

We keep a small unit test with a deliberately messy script:

raw = ("One note before the script: I could not verify the headline.\n\n# CHANNEL - BREAKING\n**HOOK**\n"
       "If verified, this is huge [S1]. (pause)\n\n## MAIN INCIDENT\nThe court sat today, according to reports.\n"
       "SOURCE NOTES\n[S1] http://example.com")
assert clean_narration(raw)["text"] == "If verified, this is huge.\n\nThe court sat today, according to reports."

Note what survives: “If verified” and “according to reports”.

Takeaways

  • Treat the text sent to TTS as its own artifact, and inspect it.
  • Strip structure, not substance: headings and stage directions go; qualifications stay.
  • Surface removed editorial notes to a human instead of deleting them.
  • Back the cleaner with an automated check after rendering.
