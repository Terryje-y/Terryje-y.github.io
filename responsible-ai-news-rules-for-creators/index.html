Responsible AI News: 8 Rules Every Creator Should Follow

AI makes news videos faster, but it can also invent quotes and facts. Eight practical rules for publishing AI-assisted news people can trust.

News AI Engine Editorial Team November 21, 2026 · 3 min read
Key takeaways
  • AI can write confident sentences that are not true. Never publish without a human check.
  • Never let AI invent quotes, decisions, numbers or evidence.
  • Match your certainty to your sources: 'reported' is not the same as 'confirmed'.

AI tools make it possible for one person to produce news videos every day. That is powerful, and it comes with responsibility. A single invented quote or wrong claim can mislead thousands of viewers, damage someone’s reputation and get your channel removed.

These are the rules we follow when building and running the News AI Engine. They apply to anyone making AI-assisted news.

Rule 1: A human chooses every story

AI should not decide what is news. A person should choose each headline, understand the story and be ready to stand behind it. In our engine the titles come from the user and are never changed by the AI.

Rule 2: Never invent quotes

Language models are very good at writing sentences that sound like something a politician or official would say. That is exactly the danger. If you cannot point to where a quote came from, it does not go in the video. Our script writer is instructed never to create quotations, and scripts are checked for them.

Rule 3: Never invent facts, decisions or evidence

The same applies to court decisions, resignations, arrests, statistics and documents. If the source does not say it, the video does not say it. When the details are not known yet, say so: “Details have not been confirmed” is honest and perfectly acceptable.

Rule 4: Match your certainty to your sources

There is a big difference between these sentences:

  • “Officials confirmed the plan.”
  • “Reports say the plan is being considered.”

If all you have is a headline, your script must not sound more certain than the headline. Our engine automatically flags phrases such as “officials confirm” or “it has been proven” when the story does not support them, and sends the script back for a rewrite.

Rule 5: Be fair to everyone in the story

Describe people and groups in neutral language. Avoid loaded words, insults and guesses about motives. If a story involves an accusation, make clear that it is an accusation.

Rule 6: Check names, numbers and dates yourself

AI can mix up similar names, swap numbers or use an old date. Before publishing, check every name, figure and date against a reliable source. This takes minutes and prevents most embarrassing mistakes.

Rule 7: Correct mistakes openly

Even careful creators make mistakes. When you do:

  • remove or correct the video quickly,
  • say what was wrong in a pinned comment or follow-up,
  • learn from it and adjust your checks.

Viewers forgive honest corrections far more readily than they forgive silence.

Rule 8: Be open about using AI

Follow each platform’s rules on labelling AI-generated or AI-assisted content. Many creators also mention their process in the channel description. Transparency builds trust; hiding it invites suspicion.

How an engine can help you stay responsible

Good automation makes responsible publishing easier, not harder. In our workflow:

  • Scripts are checked for invented quotes and unsupported certainty.
  • Nothing is posted automatically unless you choose that mode before starting.
  • A video that fails quality checks is held back from automatic posting.
  • A draft is never reported as a public post, so you always know the real status.

None of this replaces your own judgement. It simply catches the most common problems before they reach viewers.

A simple pre-publish checklist

  1. Did a person choose and understand this story?
  2. Is every quote real and sourced?
  3. Is every fact, number and date checked?
  4. Is the language as certain as the sources, and no more?
  5. Is everyone described fairly?
  6. Is AI use disclosed where required?
