From Headline to 60-Second Video: How AI Builds a Short News Clip

Follow one headline through every step of an AI video workflow, from script and voice to footage, captions and checks, and learn what to look for.

News AI Engine Editorial Team November 21, 2026 · 4 min read
Key takeaways
  • A one-minute news video needs about 150 spoken words.
  • The script, voice and captions must agree exactly, or viewers notice.
  • Always watch the first three seconds: that is where viewers decide to stay.

Short news videos look simple: one minute, one story, quick cuts and captions. Making them well every day is harder than it looks. In this guide we follow one headline through the whole process, so you know what each step does and what to look out for.

Step 1: Start with a clear headline

Everything starts with the title you choose. A good headline for short video is specific and honest. “New rules for ride-share drivers start Monday” is better than “You won’t believe what just changed”.

In our engine, the title you type is never rewritten by the AI. That matters: the headline is your promise to the viewer, and it should stay in your hands.

Step 2: Write a script the right length

A one-minute video holds roughly 150 spoken words at a natural pace. Much shorter and the video feels thin. Much longer and the voice has to rush, or the video runs over a minute.

A good short news script usually has four parts:

  • Hook (first sentence): what happened, stated plainly.
  • Context: why it is happening and who is involved.
  • What it means: the impact on ordinary people.
  • Close: what happens next or what to watch for.

The AI drafts this for you, but the script is checked automatically. If it is too short, too long, or makes claims stronger than the headline supports, it is sent back for another try.

Step 3: Turn the script into a voice

Next, an AI voice reads the script. Two things matter most here:

  • Pace. A steady, natural speed is easier to follow than a rushed one. Our engine keeps the voice at normal speed rather than speeding it up to fit more words.
  • Clean text. AI writing sometimes includes notes such as “Here is your script:” or section labels. If these reach the voice, they get read aloud. A cleaning step removes them first. We explain why in AI voiceovers for news videos.

Step 4: Choose visuals that match the words

Now the video needs pictures. A short news clip usually combines:

  • Footage cut into short clips of about three seconds, so the screen keeps moving.
  • A headline title in the first seconds so viewers know the topic immediately.
  • Key-point graphics that appear when the voice mentions an important fact or development.

The secret is timing. A graphic that appears two seconds after the voice mentions something feels wrong, even if viewers cannot say why. That is why the engine finds the exact moment each word is spoken before placing graphics.

Step 5: Add captions in sync

Most people watch short videos with the sound off, at least at first. Captions are not optional. Good captions:

  • appear in time with each spoken word,
  • are large and readable on a phone,
  • break lines in natural places, never leaving one lonely word at the end.

Read more in captions that keep viewers watching.

Step 6: Check before anything is posted

Before a video is called finished, automatic checks look for common problems:

  • Is the audio present and at a sensible volume?
  • Is the video the right length and size?
  • Do captions and graphics match the voice?
  • Is any text cut off at the edges of the screen?

If a check fails, the video is held back from automatic posting. You can still watch it, fix it or render it again.

Step 7: Save, schedule or post

Finally you decide what happens to the video. In our engine you choose one of three modes before the batch starts:

  • Render only (the default): the video is saved for you to review.
  • Upload as a draft: the video goes to your TikTok inbox to finish and post yourself.
  • Direct post: the video is posted for you, at a scheduled time, if your account allows it.

What to check yourself

Even with all of this automated, watch every video once before it goes public. Pay special attention to:

  1. The first three seconds. Is the topic clear immediately?
  2. Names and numbers. Are they correct and spelled properly?
  3. The tone. Is it fair and calm, not sensational?
